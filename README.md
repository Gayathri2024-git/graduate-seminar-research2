## 📂 Reports

### 📄 Deep Learning in Speech Recognition

This report provides a comprehensive analysis of how deep learning has revolutionized 
the field of speech recognition, transforming human-computer interaction through 
advanced neural network architectures and optimization techniques.

**Key Areas Explored:**

- **Neural Network Architectures:** In-depth coverage of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs),
  Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) units, and Transformer models
  and their specific roles in processing speech data.

- **Self-Supervised Learning:** Analysis of frameworks like Wav2Vec 2.0 that leverage large-scale
  unlabeled datasets, reducing dependency on labeled data and improving performance for 
  low-resource languages.

- **Optimization Techniques:** Examination of the Adam optimizer and its advantages in training
  deep learning models efficiently, including adaptive learning rates and faster convergence.

- **Transfer Learning:** How pretrained models such as XLS-R and Wav2Vec 2.0 are fine-tuned for
  specific languages, accents, and domain-specific applications like medical transcription.

- **Multimodal Systems:** Integration of speech with visual cues, text, and gesture recognition
  to improve accuracy in noisy and ambiguous environments.

- **Ethical AI:** Exploration of fairness, bias mitigation, data privacy, and regulatory 
  compliance (GDPR, CCPA) in speech recognition systems.

- **Hardware Advances:** Role of GPUs, TPUs, FPGAs, ASICs, and edge computing in enabling
  real-time and energy-efficient speech recognition.

- **Domain Applications:** Real-world use cases across healthcare, education, finance, 
  customer service, entertainment, and transportation.

- **Challenges:** Noisy environments, accent variability, low-resource languages, code-switching,
  real-time latency, and ethical/legal compliance.

**Key Models & Frameworks Referenced:**
`Wav2Vec 2.0` `XLS-R` `BERT` `GPT` `Transformers` `LSTMs` `Deep Belief Networks (DBNs)`

**References include:**  
Vaswani et al. (2017), Baevski et al. (2020), Kingma & Ba (2014), Devlin et al. (2018), 
Brown et al. (2020), Bengio et al. (2013)
